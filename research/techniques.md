# Rapport techniques

> Public copy of the Rapport handbook (synced from the private working folder; personal notes removed).

A working handbook for the **Rapport** bot and the **Say it** generator. Compiled 10 November 2026 (Europe/London) from Vanessa Van Edwards, Vinh Giang and Chase Hughes: their websites, books, courses, podcasts (their own and guest spots), YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, X, articles, interviews, talks and newsletters, plus the peer-reviewed research they cite. Phrases are short examples or careful adaptations of stated principles, not long excerpts from books.

**Use this to make conversations easier for the other person.** Never to type someone, pressure a yes, or run covert influence.

Companion files (same folder tree):

- `../phrases.json` — 363 generator lines (need × feeling × situation), drafted from these techniques and Cyrus's notes.
- The live generator: https://mhccog.github.io/read-anyone/ (Say it tab). Repo: https://github.com/mhccog/read-anyone

## 1. How to use this

1. **Listen first.** Pick a lean (see §6) only from what they are showing *now*. Most people show a mix. If unsure, use the "anyone" lines and ask a follow-up.
2. **Choose the feeling** you honestly want them to have (heard, understood, comfortable, liked, trusted, respected, and the fuller list in §8).
3. **Generate lines**, then say only what you mean. If you would not say it without the app, do not say it.
4. **Deliver it** with the tips in §7 (pace, pause, warmth first). The words are only half the move.
5. **Do not name the type** out loud ("you're a significance person"). Acknowledge something real that just happened.

### Evidence labels used here

| Tag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Strong** | Repeated, published research that has been checked. |
| **Moderate** | Some good research, or the coach's frame goes a little beyond it. |
| **Plausible** | Sensible and low-risk, not directly tested in our sources. |
| **Coach's view** | The coach's own method or experience. Useful, untested. Try it and judge. |

Vanessa is a strong curator of research; some of her precise numbers are illustrations (drop "82% of first impressions", "67% longer talk after a triple nod", and "60–70% eye contact" as hard facts). Vinh presents himself honestly as a practitioner; delivery can be trained, and that core claim is well supported. Chase's practical self-mastery advice is useful, but his credentials are disputed by critics, he has published no validation studies, and "read anyone in minutes" is not supported. Treat his six needs, decision styles and values as **listening prompts**, not a profile.

## 2. Ethics and what to leave out

Cyrus's own notes, and this handbook, exclude covert or manipulative technique, even when a coach teaches it:

- Embedded commands, hypnosis, "identity hacking", or making someone think an idea was theirs.
- Chase's **PCP** model (perception, context, permission) and **elicitation** by statements rather than questions, designed for intelligence work. For everyday connection, ask genuine questions (Huang et al., 2017).
- Nod-inducing hand tricks, fake stories or fake embarrassment to hold attention, compliment-then-ask-for-a-favour sequencing.
- Using the six needs, fears or decision styles to pressure, sell, or "six-minute X-ray" someone. That method has no published validation (see Myths).
- Diagnosing lies from body language. People are about 54% accurate, near chance (Bond and DePaulo, 2006).

**Sincerity test** (taught in NCI material about Chase's work, secondary source): would the person, knowing your full intent, agree that what you did was in their interest? If not, do not use the line.

## 4. Universal moves (best evidence)

These are the habits the generator should default to. They show up across the coaches *and* the research.

1. **Warmth first, then competence.** People size you up on "can I trust you?" and "can I rely on you?" (Fiske, Cuddy and Glick, 2007, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*). Vanessa's coaching frame: charisma ≈ high warmth + high competence. Competence without warmth reads as cold or suspicious (Vanessa on X, 18 Apr 2025; DOAC 2024). Lead with interest, their name, a real smile; then one clear point.
2. **Ask follow-up questions.** People who ask more questions, especially follow-ups, are liked more (Huang, Yeomans, Brooks, Minson and Gino, 2017, *JPSP*). In the speed-dating arm, high question-askers got a second-date yes 39% vs 22%. Vanessa's public advice: enter aiming to learn one thing you did not know; when they answer, ask "Wait, how did that happen?" instead of pivoting (X, 15 Oct 2026; pinned-style post 3 Sep 2026).
3. **They liked you more than you think.** The liking gap (Boothby, Cooney, Sandstrom and Clark, 2018). After a chat, say it if true: "I really enjoyed that." Send the thank-you you have been putting off (Vanessa on X).
4. **Go one level deeper than feels comfortable.** People overestimate how awkward deep conversation will feel and underestimate how good it feels (Kardas, Kumar and Epley, 2022). Swap "What do you do?" for "What's something you're working on that you actually care about?" (Vanessa, *Conversation* research guide, 2026).
5. **End on purpose.** Conversations almost never stop when both people want them to — about 2% in one large study (Mastroianni et al., 2021, *PNAS*). Close warmly: something in the future, a specific kindness, then you go.
6. **Match mode, then content.** Practical vs emotional vs social conversations (Duhigg, *Supercommunicators*, 2024, as cited by Vanessa). If they are in a feelings conversation, do not "fix" it with a plan. Vinh's simpler read: people often want information, connection, peace/harmony, or fun — match that first.
7. **Full attention cannot be faked for long.** Phone away, turn towards them, lean in a little, reflect back. High-quality listening reduces speaker anxiety and can soften extreme attitudes (Itzchakov et al., as cited in Vanessa's 2026 guide).
8. **Impressions form fast, but you cannot profile a soul.** Faces can be judged in ~100 ms (Willis and Todorov, 2006). Thin slices pick up a few visible traits, not a full profile (Ambady and Rosenthal, 1992). The "first seven seconds" is coaching shorthand.
9. **Subtle mimicry can increase liking; obvious copying backfires** (Chartrand and Bargh, chameleon effect, 1999).
10. **Labelling a feeling can reduce its intensity** (Lieberman et al., 2007). "It seems like…" then silence (Chris Voss, *Never Split the Difference* — used here as a well-evidenced adjacent tool, not one of the three coaches).
11. **Charisma can be trained** (Antonakis, Fenley and Liechti, 2011). A new habit feels fake only because it is unfamiliar (Vinh).
12. **Voice carries competence.** People who *heard* a candidate were rated more competent than people who only read the same words (Schroeder and Epley, 2015).

Dale Carnegie still holds as a short checklist: become genuinely interested, smile, remember names, be a good listener, talk in terms of their interests, make them feel important — sincerely (*How to Win Friends and Influence People*).

## 5. Techniques by coach

### 5.1 Vanessa Van Edwards

**Openers that beat autopilot** (speed-networking experiment, reported on DOAC 2026). Public *source-example* lines:

| Use | Avoid | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "What was the highlight of your day?" | "How are you?" | Breaks autopilot; asks the brain to search for something good. |
| "Working on anything exciting these days?" | "What do you do?" | Lets them talk about work *or* something better. Job-title questions can feel like "what are you worth?" |
| "Any fun plans coming up?" | | Points to a shared future. |
| "What's your personal passion project?" | | Invites a story, not a fact. |
| "What's your story?" | (too early) | Rated best *or* worst. Save it until they are comfortable. |

Also: do not open with "Nice to meet you" — it is forgettable (Instagram reel, Dec 2025). First impressions are about making people feel safe, seen and understood, not about impressing (X, 2025).

**Warmth cues (delivery):** slow triple nod at emotional moments (a fast nod reads as impatience); head tilt while listening; genuine smile that reaches the eyes; lean in; "emotional highlighter" — briefly show their feeling on your face and voice, then relax (a held face looks fake); open palm in the first seconds; show a small human imperfection early so people stop waiting for the flaw.

**Competence cues:** gestures that outline your words; vocal variety; shoulders down; look at them at the *end* of a sentence; visible hands. Steepled fingers score well in her coding but she does not use the gesture herself if it feels unnatural. Treat "60–70% eye contact" as a Western rule of thumb, not a lab number.

**If you think something nice, say it** (X, 5 Sep 2026). Specific compliments, skip the obvious, praise what they worked on (YouTube, memorable compliments). "I like you" gives people permission to be themselves. Say hi to everyone (X, 2025). Being likable makes you more credible; warmth makes people trust your judgement (TikTok).

**Curious before critical:** when someone frustrates you, ask what explanation you would give if you had done the same thing (TikTok).

**Exit:** leave them feeling valued. Mention the future, a specific kindness, then go.

**Conversation Blueprint (2026 book):** Level 1 — put people at ease (safety + warmth in the first moments); Level 2 — connect more deeply; Level 3 — create meaning. Each level has three power questions (all nine in `techniques-inventory.md`). Earlier public substitutes, still valid: highlight / exciting work / fun plans (ease); a sincere follow-up and "what's something you're working on that you care about?" (deeper); later, meaning-level questions only when the relationship can hold them.

**Assume they already like you** a little more than you fear (liking gap + her "power of assumption" post). Practise small weekly social reps.

### 5.2 Vinh Giang

**Voice as an instrument.** Melody, rate, volume, tonality, pause. Slow down to highlight; speed up for energy. Face is the remote control for emotion in the voice. End key sentences on a slightly lower pitch (sounds more sure — TikTok). Swap "um" for a pause. Record five minutes off the cuff, wait a day, review three ways (audio, muted video, transcript), pick *one* habit a week.

**If it feels fake, say "unfamiliar" and keep going.** Four stages of competence: the clunky third stage is where people quit.

**Gestures in the "power sphere"** (navel to eyes, elbows off the sides; from Mark Bowden). Palms up to welcome, palms down for a firm point (pitch often drops with palms down).

**Match, Mirror and Lead (MML)** — official blog:

1. Match and mirror vocal foundations, body language and tone (if they are quiet and slow, lower and slow down; they relax).
2. Lead gently once you are in sync — a question, a slightly lighter tone, a decision. Do not yank the energy up on a nervous person.

Spot what they want from *this* conversation: information (be clear and short), connection (warmth), harmony/peace, or fun, then tailor.

**Phrases and games (*source-example* or close paraphrase):**

- High / Low / Buffalo: each person shares a high, a low, and something unusual. Six threads instead of one shot in the dark.
- "Yes, and…" keeps a conversation open; "yes, but…" wipes out what came before (Instagram, Jun 2025).
- "Tell me more" buys time and makes them feel heard.
- "The one thing is…" (or 3-2-1: three steps, two types, or the one thing) when you are put on the spot.
- FORD topics: Family, Occupation, Recreation, Dreams. Favourite: "What do you do in your free time?"
- Listen for "threads": a flash of emotion, folded arms, a shift in tone, and gently follow them (TikTok).
- Playfulness is an underrated connection skill (TikTok).

**On video:** camera on at hello, show torso and hands, good light and mic, a little more energy ("generosity of energy").

**Gears, not one gear.** Calm composure as the base (this also fits Chase); turn energy up when the moment needs it, always matched to them first.

### 5.3 Chase Hughes (coach's view unless noted)

**Composure before scripts.** ACSS: Authority, Comfort, Social skills, Skills. Be the most comfortable person in the room. Collapse (shrinking) and posturing (puffing up) are the two traps. Move a little slower than the room; fear speeds the body up.

**Authority** rests on life order (environment, time, appearance, social life, finances) plus inner qualities: confidence, discipline, leadership, gratitude and enjoyment. Fake posture will not last. "Enjoyment" of the present is, in his words, highly magnetic. **Strong research does not validate this model;** it is low-risk self-work.

**The five Cs before you read a cue:** Change from their baseline, Context, Clusters, Culture, Checklist last. A shift means something changed, usually stress or discomfort, not a lie.

**Ask, don't diagnose.** "Is this a comfortable area to talk about?" Hear the feeling under the words ("you never call" is often about feeling unappreciated).

**Six social needs** — listening prompts only (see §6). In the 2026 DOAC episode he says the most important people skill is making people feel **heard and seen**, resonating without judging, and that this will matter more as AI grows.

**Six decision styles** (people often use two neighbouring ones): deviance, novelty, social, conformity, necessity, investment.

**Six values** (listen for "so I can finally…"): connection, information, recognition, experience, freedom, growth.

Do not run a "six-minute X-ray." Brief impressions are not a full read.

**Needs-based acknowledgement rules** (NCI secondary write-up, not Chase's own page): relevant to what just happened, socially normal, imply the quality rather than name it, and be sincere.

## 6. The six needs — listening prompts for the generator

Never a stamp. Primary: Significance, Approval, Acceptance. Secondary: Intelligence, Hardship (Chase says "pity"), Power (sometimes "strength").

| Need | Quiet question | Listen for | Speak to | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Significance** | Do others see me as making an impact? | What they built, scale, "I built that", standing out | Name the specific impact, then a follow-up: "What are you proudest of?" | Vague "that's amazing"; stealing the story |
| **Approval** | Am I doing this right? | Checks in, "is that okay?", plays themselves down | Clear, specific validation: what was good and why | Vague praise; shoving them to be bolder |
| **Acceptance** | Do I belong? | We / us / our, the group, who is in or out | Shared language, make room, talk about the we | Making it all about you; leaving them on the edge |
| **Intelligence** | Do others see me as sharp? | Expertise, method, "walk me through", rich vocabulary | Ask for their expert view and mean it | Talking over them; dumbing it down |
| **Hardship** | Do others realise how hard this has been? | What they have been through; "nobody sees" | Confirm it was hard, pause, then gently move on | "It's not that bad"; instant fixing |
| **Power** | Do I have the final say? | Directs, wants the headline, "here's how we're doing it" | Short options, they decide | Long preambles; wrestling for control |

Vinh's coarser map (information / connection / harmony / fun) can sit on top of this when the need is unclear.

Vanessa also describes personality "superpower" sketches in course material (ambitious hero, contented pragmatist, selfless helper, creative disruptor, unlucky struggler). Use only as colour, not as a second typing system.

## 7. Delivery tips (attach to generated lines)

Reuse these rather than inventing new ones. Sources: Vinh (VG), Vanessa (VVE), Chase (CH, coach's view).

| Id | Tip | Use when they should feel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| slownod | A slow nod at emotional moments. Fast nods feel like "hurry up." | Heard, understood |
| tilt | Slight head tilt while they talk — "tell me more." | Heard, liked |
| highlight | Briefly show their feeling on your face and voice, then relax. | Understood |
| lean | Lean in a little when they say something that matters. | Important, curious, included |
| smile | A relaxed smile that reaches the eyes. | Liked, included |
| soft | Drop your volume a touch. People often relax when you do. | Comfortable, reassured |
| match | Start near their pace and volume, then lead a notch at a time. Obvious copying backfires. | Comfortable, liked, trusted |
| pause | Pause just before and after the key words. | Heard, important, trusted |
| slowdown | Slow right down on the one line that matters. | Trusted, reassured |
| melody | Add melody; end the key sentence on a slightly lower note. | Trusted, respected, in control |
| palmsup | Open, visible palms to invite them in. | Comfortable, included, in control |
| palmsdown | Palms down for a firm, calm point. | Respected |
| askstop | Ask, then stop talking. Let the silence work. | Smart, in control, curious |
| computer | Hand to chin or a slight tilt — you are taking it in. | Heard, smart |
| warm | Keep the same warm tone through the hard part. | Reassured, reconciled, appreciated |
| congruent | Face matches the message: serious when serious, bright for good news. | Trusted, reconciled |

**Chase, coach's view:** say a price, opinion or request once, calmly, then stop. Pool-speed movement before you walk in.

## 8. Generator specification

**Inputs**

1. Need: significance, approval, acceptance, intelligence, pity (shown as Hardship), power, or `any`.
2. Feeling: heard, understood, liked, trusted, respected, comfortable, reassured, included, important, smart, control (in control), reconciled, appreciated, curious.
3. Situation (optional): opener, rapport, disagree, close. (Everyday context — stranger, networking, business, family, online — can flavour examples but is not required in `phrases.json`.)

**Matching rule**

1. Exact need + feeling + situation.
2. Same need + feeling, any situation.
3. `any` need + same feeling + situation.
4. `any` need + same feeling.
5. Same need, closest feeling.
6. Always return at least three lines; label looser matches "more general."

**Copy and memory.** One-tap copy. Favourites saved in `localStorage`. No cloud, no API key, no LLM in v1.

**Guardrails in the UI**

- Short line: "Listening prompts, not a label. Only say what you mean."
- No covert techniques (see §2).
- If need is unknown, start with `any` and a follow-up question.

**Feelings for a simple first screen:** heard, understood, comfortable, liked, trusted, respected. Offer "more" for the rest.

## 9. Ready-to-use public lines (short)

Use freely; these were given as examples or are tight paraphrases of a stated principle.

**Anyone / openers**

- "What was the highlight of your day?"
- "Working on anything exciting these days?"
- "Any fun plans coming up?"
- "What do you do in your free time?"
- "What was that like?" / "How did that happen?" / "Tell me more."
- "I really enjoyed that." (only if true)

**Disagree and close**

- "Yes, and…"
- "I think we both want…"
- "It's been lovely talking. Good luck with [specific thing]. I'm going to [exit]."
- "I kept thinking about what you said about [specific thing]."

**Need-coloured (adapt, be sincere)**

- Significance: "That took real building. What are you proudest of?"
- Approval: "You handled that well — especially [the specific bit]."
- Acceptance: "You are not crashing it. Come in — we were just talking about…"
- Intelligence: "I'd like your read on this — you know this terrain."
- Hardship: "That sounds like a heavy stretch. I'm glad you said it."
- Power: "Two options. You choose which we run with."

## 10. Myths to drop

- **"93% of communication is non-verbal."** A misreading of Mehrabian. He said it does not apply to everyday conversation. Words matter.
- **You can profile needs, fears and decision style in a few minutes.** Not supported. Ambady and Rosenthal: thin slices pick up a little, not everything.
- **You can spot liars from microexpressions or one cue.** About 54%, near chance (Bond and DePaulo, 2006). Training in microexpressions did not reliably improve lie detection (Jordan et al., 2019).
- **Power poses change your hormones.** Did not replicate (Ranehill et al., 2015). Open posture may help you feel a bit steadier.
- **Hold loosely** exact figures: 60–70% eye contact, 82% warmth and competence, 150–180 words per minute, "67% longer" after a triple nod. Treat as illustrations.

## 11. Practice loop (for the bot and the site)

Daily: pick today's lean, run three cards, then use it in one real conversation. Weekly focus, one at a time (Vinh): (1) two follow-ups before you tell your story; (2) warmth first, one clear point, end on purpose; (3) match then lead; (4) slow down on the line that matters, send two sincere "I enjoyed…" messages.
